# H100 compressed-P32 M960 five-row reuse (2026-09-23 UTC)

The M960 Hopper direct-P32 path now decodes each compressed weight fragment
once for five M16 activation tiles on wide Llama 3.2 1B Q, gate, and down
projections. K/V stay at four rows because five-row reuse slowed the narrow
K projection in the matched kernel gate. The full-model 2x prefill objective
remains open; this is a measured incremental improvement, not a 2x claim.

## Contract and selection

- Raw ABI v3, algorithm 5, FP16 activation, contiguous P32 window, FP32 inner
  output, unsplit K reduction, BM80/BN64/BK256 for five rows versus BM64 for
  four, two TMA stages, sm_90a. BM64/BN128 adds two N64 consumers only for
  W3 gate/down. Other BN128 and BM80 combinations fail closed.
- M=960 and one projection. W2 Q: K2048/N2048 uses five rows. W2.5 K:
  K2048/N512 stays four. W3.5 V: K2048/N512 stays four. W3 gate:
  K2048/N8192 and down K8192/N2048 use five.
- M960 has 60 M16 tiles. Five rows/CTA yields `grid.y=12`; four rows yields
  `grid.y=15`. The requested `block_m` selects the matching compiled
  specialization in both QVQ's raw launch and launch-plan entries. BN128
  halves `grid.x` and uses 288 threads/CTA versus BN64's 160.
- Packed weights, level table, bank selectors, decoded FP16 values, per-row
  WGMMA accumulation order, rank-8 correction, and output recovery are unchanged.
  No dense-weight cache or external workspace is introduced.
- The last tested immutable ZML QVQ pin was
  `334a9ec80e31594896ab45982ce07f29f85021da`. The five-row QVQ payload is
  **not pinned in ZML yet**; do not call it a production default until that
  consumer pin and automatic dispatch are validated.

## W3 funnel shift and BN128 follow-up

The proposed W3 `uint64` window extraction to `__funnelshift_r` rewrite was
bitwise exact but did **not** improve generated code: matched R5 gate SASS
executed the same 82,343,448 instructions, including 8,348,160 `SHF`
instructions, with 107 registers/thread and 111.36 KB dynamic shared/CTA in
both arms. A 100-round paired gate probe was 171.17 us control versus
172.16 us candidate. The source rewrite was reverted. Reports:
`row5-w3-u64-control-gate.ncu-rep` and
`row5-w3-funnelshift-gate.ncu-rep` under
`/root/qvq-profiler-artifacts/m960-rows5-20260923/`.

BN128/R4 does raise scheduler supply on W3 gate: achieved occupancy
15.00% to 26.33% and eligible warps/scheduler 0.76 to 1.49 versus R5/BN64.
It uses 93 registers/thread, 107.26 KB dynamic shared/CTA, fits two CTAs/SM,
and has zero local/shared spills. However, its 95.59M executed instructions
and 181.12 us matched median are worse than R5/BN64's 82.34M instructions
and 171.78 us. Occupancy was not the gate-projection objective; R5 stays the
faster gate route. BN128/R4 was 181.73 us versus R4/BN64's 194.85 us, so
the two-consumer hypothesis helps only relative to the four-row baseline.

For W3 down (M960/K8192/N2048), BN128/R4 is a small positive candidate:
two 100-round paired real-metadata gates measured R5/BN64 176.42/175.78 us
versus BN128/R4 174.72/174.08 us, both bitwise exact. This is a 0.97% local
gain, **not** a measured full-model gain. The new ABI form remains explicit;
ZML must select and validate it separately before promotion. Final raw-library
SHA-256: `5f2f938cba2c789423ea480a7180b4d1590780ce04fc2b58069e8d269aa9f6ce`.
BN128 reports: `row4-bn128-gate.ncu-rep` (earlier equivalent device build)
and `row4-bn128-down-final.ncu-rep` (final library) in the same artifact
directory. The final down profile measured 23.93% achieved occupancy,
1.42 eligible warps/scheduler, 93.11M executed instructions and zero spills.
The final BM80 gate profile (`row5-bm80-final-gate.ncu-rep`) retained
82.34M executed instructions, 107 registers/thread, zero spills and 15.01%
achieved occupancy. A post-profile 100-round paired run against the retained
R5-only binary was bitwise exact and 171.17/171.30 us (control/final),
consistent with no meaningful change to the selected R5 route.
